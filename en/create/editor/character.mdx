## What it controls

A character node establishes someone the player can meet. The portrait makes the person recognizable; deeper settings keep their behavior and speech consistent; act goals give them a reason to act.

In **Letter from Mist Harbor**, the main non-player character is **Lin Zhou**, the night clerk at the Tidebound Post Office.

## Basic profile

| Field | What to write | Example | Player sees it where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name | A stable, recognizable name | `Lin Zhou` | Dialogue, character menu, and details |
| Identity | The character’s relationship to the story | `Night clerk at the Tidebound Post Office` | Character details and introductions |
| Gender | Optional profile information | `Male` | Character details when filled |
| Age | A detail consistent with the character’s life | `32` | Character details |
| Act portraits | Images for meaningful changes | `Work coat in the post office; windblown collar at the old pier` | Scene, dialogue avatar, and details |

Keep the name and identity consistent, and enter age as digits only (for example `32`); “32 years old” or a spelled-out number fails validation. Add a new portrait only when the character’s state or time really changes.

### How to fill the basic profile

- Keep the name consistent; do not change how the character is addressed between acts;
- Write the identity as a relationship the player can understand, not an internal shorthand only the author knows;
- Match age to the character's experience and way of speaking;
- Add a portrait only when the character's state or time really changes, not one per act by default.

### Portrait specs and production

Portraits appear in scenes, dialogue avatars, and character details. Besides a default portrait, you can set a portrait for a specific act; an act without its own portrait falls back to the default.

- **Size and scaling**: vertical `768×1024`. The editor scales to that size on upload; when the ratio does not match, it fits the whole image inside and pads the rest with transparency.
- **Frame**: a **mid-shot from the top of the head to mid-thigh**; do not draw below the knees or use a full-body image. Do not crop the top of the head, arms, or upper-body clothing.
- **Background and transparency**: use a `PNG` or `WebP` with a real transparent background (alpha channel), not a white, green-screen, or scene background; the editor does not remove backgrounds for you.
- **Format and size**: `PNG`, `JPEG`, or `WebP`, under `4MB`; prefer `PNG` or `WebP` when transparency must be preserved.
- **Cross-act consistency**: keep identity, face, hair, and clothing cues consistent across acts, and change only the confirmed outfit, light, or pose.
- **Prompt**: state a real transparent background, the mid-shot framing, and nothing below the knees, and exclude green screen, white background, scene, ground shadow, text, watermark, and extra characters.

## Deeper settings

<Warning>
  These deeper settings apply to non-player characters only. If the character is selected as the protagonist in [Script Entry](/en/create/editor/start), the platform removes core desire, breaking point, usual methods, public persona, and the three speaking-style fields on save, keeping only name, identity, gender, age, and act portraits. Put protagonist behavior rules in story control instead.
</Warning>

These guide the story but are not displayed as editor instructions:

| Field | Example for Lin Zhou |
| --- | --- |
| Core desire | `Protect a delivery register that cannot be made public.` |
| Boundary and breaking point | `He will not let anyone burn the undelivered letter.` |
| Usual method | `First confirm what the other person saw, then answer one question.` |
| Public persona | `Quiet, polite, always straightening the desk.` |
| Normal speech | `Short sentences; he looks toward the door before answering.` |
| Under pressure | `Covers the register before explaining himself.` |
| After breaking | `Says directly that the harbor must be left before high tide.` |

Write behaviors a player can notice rather than labels such as “brave” or “mysterious.”

## Act goals

Fill a goal for each act the character appears in. For **Tide Before Midnight**, an example goal is: `Confirm whether Shen Yao noticed the salt mark, then decide whether to hand over the old-pier key.` Act goals are background fields: the player never sees them as a task list, but they keep the character's direction stable across different conversations in the same act, so the character does not forget the problem they are trying to solve.

## From fields to the game page

1. **Name, identity, age, and gender** let the player recognize the person in the character menu;
2. **The portrait** lets the player see the person in scenes and beside dialogue;
3. **Deeper settings** show through the character's dialogue, actions, and reactions under pressure;
4. If the character is bound to a visible state component, its name and current value appear in the character details;
5. Whether the character can appear in an act also depends on binding them to that act; creating the node alone does not put them on stage.

## Check it

- Name and age are filled in;
- The profile details the player needs do not contradict each other;
- For non-player characters, deeper settings describe behavior and reactions, not vague adjectives;
- The character is bound to an act where the story actually happens;
- At least one test confirmed how the character appears in the character menu and beside dialogue.
