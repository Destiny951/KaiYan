## What it controls

An act is a stage of the story. It limits which characters and locations may appear, states what changes, and explains when the stage can end.

In **Letter from Mist Harbor**, the first act is **Tide Before Midnight**. It takes the player from receiving the letter to confirming that it came from someone still in the harbor.

## Act core

| Field | What to write | Example | Player sees the result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Act name | A name that makes the stage easy to identify | `Tide Before Midnight` | Usually not shown as a page title |
| Main route | The previous and next connection | `Entry → Tide Before Midnight → Whether to Visit the Old Pier` | The player enters the connected stage |
| Available characters | People who may appear in this act | `Lin Zhou` | The player can meet these characters |
| Available locations | Places allowed in the act | `Tidebound Post Office` | The player can use the location |
| Progress direction | The causal change this stage must create | `Move from receiving the letter to confirming the sender is still in the harbor.` | Appears through scenes and actions |
| Closure basis | Observable facts and what the next stage inherits | `The salt mark is found; the next stage inherits the old-pier direction.` | The story moves on without inventing a jump |

### Write the progress direction

Split it into two parts:

1. **Core change**: what fact does the player or the world gain by the end of this act?
2. **Change direction**: through which different actions can the player approach that change, and what stays up to the player?

### Write the closure basis

Use three parts: **normal closure** (what the player can observe when the act ends normally), **early closure** (which facts can end it early on a different choice, and which cannot be pretended to have happened), and **inherited content** (the location, items, clues, intelligence, and state the next act must carry).

“Task complete” is not enough; state what the player can actually observe.

## Foreshadowing

| Field | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Foreshadowing name | `Salt mark on the envelope` |
| Content | `Salt appears only on the edge facing the old pier.` |
| Effect | `At the old pier, the tide mark matches the envelope.` |

- **Foreshadowing name**: a short identifier for cross-act lookup, distinguishable at a glance;
- **Content**: the concrete detail the player **can actually reach** when it is planted (an object, an action, one line); do not write the full truth yet;
- **Effect**: which facts, relationships, or resources change once it is detonated.

Foreshadowing runs as a plant-then-detonate loop: define it in an act's **Foreshadowing**, then mark it as **planted** in an event; detonation never happens on its own, so a later event must explicitly **detonate** it to close the loop. Every planted foreshadowing needs a detonation or a clear reason to stay open, or the player reads a thread that never pays off.

## Events inside an act

### Common fields

| Field | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Event summary | `Lin Zhou denies seeing the letter; the player checks the register; the time of delivery is confirmed.` |
| Suggested turns | `2–5 turns` |
| People | `Lin Zhou` |
| Location | `Tidebound Post Office` |
| Narrative intent | `Show that the letter was deliberately placed.` |
| Entry situation | `The player holds the letter in the dark lobby.` |
| Required question | `Was it placed tonight?` |
| Allowed result boundary | `Confirm delivery time, but do not reveal the sender’s identity yet.` |

These are background fields. The player experiences the result through dialogue, observations, clues, and changed access—not through the field labels.

### Fine control

Break the event into consecutive story steps, each with:

- **Step name**, such as “Confirm the power switch location”;
- **The narrative change this step completes**, such as “the player knows the broadcast equipment is behind the waiting room”;
- **Direction guidance and boundaries**, such as “the player may investigate the platform first, but a single guess must not count as repairing the equipment”;
- **Driving source**: which character, environment, item, or prior fact pushes it.

Each step makes one main change, so the player has a chance to observe and respond.

<Note>
  Required fields: title, act goal, act resolution, and act events. Missing values block testing and review; see [Required Fields and Blockers](/en/create/editor/required-fields).
</Note>

## Background fields and the player

The act name, connections, character and location selection, progress direction, closure basis, foreshadowing, and event fields are all background direction. The player never sees their text; what they see is the current location description and scene image, character lines and reactions, facts discovered through observation and action, and the location, items, clues, and state carried into the next act. Write player-facing sentences into story background, opening text, location description, clue content, character profile, or ending description—not into act fields.

## Player-facing check

If the act allows a player-visible location, character, item, clue, intelligence, or state, open the corresponding player page in a test session and confirm when it appears. The act name alone is not enough to explain what the player sees.
