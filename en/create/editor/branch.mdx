## What it controls

A branch is a route decision in the editor, not a button that players see. Use it when an earlier act should lead to different next steps based on facts, a player decision, or a current state.

The example branch is **Whether to Visit the Old Pier**. One route follows the clue to the pier; the other leaves a reply at the post office.

| Field | What to write | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Branch name | A useful label for the canvas | `Whether to Visit the Old Pier` |
| Exit route strategy | The established fact for each connection | `The player has found the salt mark and clearly chooses to visit the old pier.` |

Connect one incoming route and at least two outgoing routes. Write a separate, observable condition for each route. Good conditions say what the player did or what fact is true; they do not say “if the player is clever.”

## How to build a branch

1. Connect one incoming route from the previous act;
2. Connect the branch to two or more later acts or endings;
3. Write a separate condition for each outgoing route;
4. Check that the routes differ and that no route always leads to the same place;
5. Trigger every route in a test session and confirm the player page really differs.

## Write facts, not vibes

A condition states what has already happened, not what would be more exciting:

- **Good**: `The player found the salt mark and clearly chooses to visit the old pier.`
- **Good**: `The player leaves the letter at the post office and chooses to wait.`
- **Avoid**: `If the player is clever, go to the good ending.`
- **Avoid**: `Choose the most suitable route from the player's tone.`

When a condition depends on a clue, intelligence, or state, say whether the player saw the evidence or already understood the fact; do not treat the two as the same.

## Common branch mistakes

- No incoming or no outgoing route;
- An outgoing route has no condition, so a test cannot tell why it was taken;
- Two routes describe the same result with different titles;
- A fact is written as already true before it happens;
- The branch is used as an ending, so the story stops before the player is done.

## What the player sees

The branch card and strategy text are hidden. The player sees different dialogue, location access, items, clues, intelligence, or later endings. If you want a choice to be explicit, write it as a natural action or conversation in the player scene.

<Note>
  A branch has no required fields, but every exit route needs a selection rule; without one, a test cannot tell why the route was taken. See [Required Fields and Blockers](/en/create/editor/required-fields).
</Note>

## Check it in testing

Trigger every route from a test session. Make sure the routes actually differ, no route has an unconnected exit, and a future fact has not been written as if it already happened.
