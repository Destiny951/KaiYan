## What it controls

An item needs more than a name. The player should know what it looks like, why it matters, and what meaningful interaction it supports.

In **Letter from Mist Harbor**, the item is **Undelivered Letter**. Its salt mark points toward a clue without naming the sender.

## Basic fields

| Field | What to write | Example | Player sees it where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Item name | A recognizable inventory name | `Undelivered Letter` | Inventory, item details, and story text |
| Ownership | The character holding it or the scene it sits in, choose one | `Player` | It controls whether the item enters the inventory |
| Appearance | Details the player can observe | `The envelope edge is water-softened; a thin salt ring marks the seal.` | Item details |
| Image | A visual identifier | `Old envelope with a salt mark` | Inventory tile and item details |
| Acquisition | When and how it is obtained | `Inspect the register desk and choose to take the letter.` | It controls when the item appears |
| Interactions | What the player can do with it | `Inspect the salt mark or show it to Lin Zhou.` | Results appear in the story, not as editor rules |

### Item image specs and production

An item image appears in the inventory tile and item details.

- **Size and scaling**: square `512×512`. The editor scales to that size on upload; when the ratio does not match, it fits the whole image inside and pads the rest with transparency.
- **Frame**: a complete, clear subject away from the edges, and not a tiny subject filling the frame with empty space.
- **Background**: transparency is not required. Prefer a transparent background when the item appears on its own in the inventory or UI; for a scene-based item image, use a full background consistent with the act.
- **Format and size**: `PNG`, `JPEG`, or `WebP`, under `4MB`, with a source close to `512×512`.
- **Consistency**: keep medium, material, and brushwork consistent across items in the script; a scene-based image follows the act's light and environment without changing confirmed identifying features.
- **Prompt**: state which item this is, a complete square subject, and the confirmed shape and material, and exclude text, watermarks, and extra subjects.

## Write acquisition and interaction

### Acquisition

Describe where, through which action, and with what result. For example:

> `The player inspects the register desk at the post office, finds the undelivered letter, and chooses to take it.`

Do not write “it appears when the story needs it.” The player must be able to obtain the item through observation, conversation, or action.

### Interactions

Give the item one or two meaningful uses first:

- **Inspect**: reveals a new detail;
- **Compare**: connects it to a location or clue;
- **Hand over**: changes a character’s attitude or a route;
- **Use**: makes a condition true, but does not finish the story by itself.

Interactions are background rules. The player sees the resulting text, character response, or change—not the field text.

<Note>
  Required field: item name. Ownership is a relation, not text: a character holds the item or it sits in a scene, never both. An item with neither never enters any act and blocks testing and review. See [Required Fields and Blockers](/en/create/editor/required-fields).
</Note>

## Check it

The name and image should identify the item, the appearance should contain observable details, and the acquisition action should be possible in a test session. Confirm that the item enters inventory and that its details or interaction appear as expected. Connect ownership first: an item is held by a character or placed in a scene.
